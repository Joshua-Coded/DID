Student Activity Guide

Live DID Simulation

Veramo CLI + Universal Resolver

Week 4, Session 2 Blockchain & Identity Management LLPS

What You Will Do

In this activity you will create a real Decentralized Identifier (DID) on the Ethereum Sepolia blockchain, resolve it, issue a Verifiable Credential, and validate it — all from your terminal. By the end you will have personally experienced what self-sovereign identity feels like.

  • Create your own DID on a real blockchain network
  • Resolve and inspect your DID Document
  • Issue a Verifiable Credential linked to your DID
  • Validate your DID using the Universal Resolver

Part 1
Install Veramo & Create Your DID

Step 1 Install Veramo CLI

Open your terminal and run the following command:

$ npm i @veramo/cli -g

Then verify installation with:

$ veramo --version
Expected output: A version number like 7.0.0 — if you see this, you are ready.

Step 2 Create a Project Folder & Config

$ mkdir my-did-project $ cd my-did-project $ veramo config create
What this does: Creates an agent.yml file — this is your local DID agent configuration. It sets up your key manager, DID providers, and database.

Step 3 Create Your DID

$ veramo did create

You will be prompted with three questions:

PromptYour Answer
Select identifier provider choose did:ethr:sepolia
Select key management system choose local
Enter alias press Enter to skip

You should see output like this:

┌──────────────────┬───────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ provider │ alias │ did │ ├──────────────────┼───────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ did:ethr:sepolia │ │ did:ethr:sepolia:0x02f25fe5c52e452055436f... │ └──────────────────┴───────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
Congratulations! You just created a real Decentralized Identifier on the Ethereum Sepolia blockchain. Nobody issued it to you. You own it.

Record your DID here — you will need it for every step below:

did:ethr:sepolia:0x ___________________________________________
Part 2
Resolve & Inspect Your DID Document

Step 4 Resolve Your DID via Terminal

Copy your full DID string from Step 3 and run:

$ veramo did resolve did:ethr:sepolia:0x<YOUR_DID_HERE>

You will see a full JSON DID Document with your verification methods, authentication keys, and context. This is the identity document that lives on-chain.

Step 5 Resolve Your DID via Universal Resolver (Browser)

  1. Open this URL: https://dev.uniresolver.io
  2. Paste your full DID string (did:ethr:sepolia:0x...) into the input field
  3. Click Resolve
  4. Inspect the DID Document that appears

Look for these fields in the result:

FieldWhat it means
idYour unique DID string
verificationMethodYour cryptographic public keys
authenticationProof that you can sign with this identity
assertionMethodProof that credentials you issue are valid
Think about this: All of this data is public, but nobody can modify it without your private key. What does that mean for identity security?
Part 3
Issue a Verifiable Credential

Step 6 Create a Verifiable Credential

Now you will issue a credential — this is like a blockchain-signed certificate attached to your DID. Run:

$ veramo credential create

Answer the prompts like this:

PromptYour Answer
Issuer DIDYour full DID from Step 3
Credential proofFormatlds
Subject DIDYour full DID again (you are issuing to yourself)
Credential TypeVerifiableCredential,Profile
Claim Typename
Claim ValueYour actual name
What just happened? You issued a cryptographically signed credential proving your name is linked to your DID. No university, no government, no bank needed to verify you — the blockchain does it.
Part 4
Explore the Veramo Explorer

Step 7 Launch the Visual Explorer

Veramo has a built-in visual interface. Run:

$ veramo explore

This opens a browser dashboard where you can see all your DIDs, credentials, and messages in a visual interface. Browse around and explore what you created.

Reflection Questions

Answer these after completing the activity. Be ready to share with the class.

  • When you created your DID, who gave you permission to do that?
  • Look at your DID Document on the Universal Resolver. What information is public? What is private?
  • Your credential says your name is linked to your DID. Could someone fake this? Why or why not?
  • Think of a farmer or a refugee with no government ID. How would giving them a DID change their access to services?
  • What happens if you lose your private key? What does that mean for self-sovereign identity?

Bonus Challenge

  • Issue a credential to a classmate's DID instead of your own
